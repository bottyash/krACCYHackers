# MediRoute Emergency Triage Protocol — Reference v2.1

## Priority Levels

### P1 — Immediate (Red)
Life-threatening. Requires intervention within 0–10 minutes.
Indicators:
- Airway compromise or SpO2 < 90% with distress
- Hemodynamic instability: SBP < 90, HR > 130 or < 40
- Active ST-elevation MI (STEMI) or high-suspicion ACS with instability
- GCS ≤ 8 or rapidly declining
- Anaphylaxis, septic shock, haemorrhagic shock
- Suspected ectopic pregnancy with haemodynamic compromise
- Meningococcal septicaemia (non-blanching rash + fever + shock)
- Active stroke within thrombolysis window (< 4.5 hours onset)
- Thunderclap headache with meningism (subarachnoid haemorrhage suspected)
- Paediatric: any of above adapted for age-appropriate vitals

### P2 — Urgent (Orange)
Potentially life-threatening. Requires assessment within 10–30 minutes.
Indicators:
- Chest pain, stable vitals, no STEMI but cardiac markers pending
- Stroke symptoms outside thrombolysis window or unknown onset
- Severe abdominal pain with signs of peritonism
- Acute biliary colic with fever and abnormal LFTs
- Moderate respiratory distress: SpO2 93–96%, RR 20–24
- Fever > 39.0°C with systemic signs in adult
- Significant trauma, suspected fractures with neurovascular compromise

### P3 — Less Urgent (Yellow)
Requires assessment within 30–60 minutes.
Indicators:
- Minor infections without systemic involvement
- Uncomplicated UTI in otherwise well patient
- Stable limb injury, able to partially weight bear
- Mild pain (< 5/10), non-specific symptoms, stable vitals
- Known chronic condition exacerbation, sub-acute presentation

### P4 — Non-Urgent (Green)
Can wait > 60 minutes. Suitable for minor injuries unit or GP referral.
Indicators:
- Minor cuts, abrasions
- Mild pharyngitis, common cold symptoms
- Medication queries, repeat prescriptions
- No abnormal vitals, no acute distress

## Key Scoring Tools

### FAST (Stroke)
- Face droop
- Arm weakness
- Speech disturbance
- Time of onset

### Sepsis Red Flags (Adult)
- Temperature > 38.3 or < 36
- HR > 90, RR > 20
- Altered mental status
- Suspected infection source
- Lactate > 2 mmol/L → sepsis; > 4 → septic shock

### AVPU / GCS
- Alert (GCS 15)
- Voice (GCS ~13)
- Pain (GCS ~8)
- Unresponsive (GCS < 8) → P1

## Paediatric Vitals Reference (Age 5–12)
- HR: 80–120 bpm (normal); >140 = elevated
- SBP normal: 85 + (2 × age in years) mmHg; SBP <80 = hypotensive
- RR: 18–25 (normal); >30 = elevated
- SpO2: ≥ 95% normal; < 94% = concerning
